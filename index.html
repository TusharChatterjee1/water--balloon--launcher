AP Physics 1 · Units 1 to 4

Same pullback, same landing spot.

We launched water balloons with a resistance band held by two people. It was hard to hit the target because every shot came out different. This site uses our launcher to explain kinematics, forces, energy, and momentum. Each unit has a lab you can run yourself.

Both launches used a 1.8 m pullback. The speeds should match, but they are 3 m/s apart. Our spring constants were also all over the place: 93, 88, 30, and 57 N/m.

Our modification

A fixed frame with a pullback stop

The problem is how the band is held. Two people hold it at different heights, and they get pulled forward when the band snaps. So the height, direction, and stretch change every shot.

Our fix is a 5 ft wooden frame. The band is tied to the tops of two posts, so it is always at the same height and width. Distance markers on the base rails and a small wooden peg set the pullback. The pouch is pulled back to the peg, so every shot from the same marker has the same stretch.

Unit 1

Kinematics

After the balloon leaves the band, it is a projectile. Its motion splits into two parts that do not affect each other. Sideways, nothing pushes it, so vx stays the same. Up and down, gravity changes vy by 9.8 m/s every second.

This is why the frame helps. If the launch speed v0 and the angle θ0 are the same every shot, the path is the same every shot, and we can predict the landing distance Δx.

Equations

v0x = v0 cos θ0    v0y = v0 sin θ0

Δx = v0x t

Δy = v0y t + ½at²  with a = −g

vfy = v0y + at

H = h + v0y² / (2g)

Unit 2

Forces

The band acts like a spring. The more it is stretched, the harder it pulls. If the pullback x1 is the same every time, the force on the balloon is the same every time. The peg makes that happen.

The band also pulls on the frame with the same size force in the opposite direction (Newton's third law). When people held the band, that force pulled their hands around. The braces and stakes push back on the frame, so its net force is zero and it does not move.

Equations

Fs = k x1  spring force

Fg = mg  weight

ΣF = ma  Newton's second law

Fband on frame = −Fframe on band

Unit 3

Energy

Pulling the band back stores elastic energy. When we let go, that energy turns into kinetic energy. On the way up, some kinetic energy turns into gravitational energy, and on the way down it turns back. The total stays the same the whole time.

A fixed pullback means the same stored energy every shot. The same stored energy means the same launch speed. That is the main reason the peg makes the launcher more accurate.

Equations

Eel,i + KEi + PEi = Eel,f + KEf + PEf

Eel = ½kx1²   KE = ½mv²   PE = mgh

½kx1² = ½mv0²  band to launch

v0 = x1√(k/m)

Unit 4

Momentum

The balloon starts with zero momentum. The band pushes it for a short time, and that push is an impulse. The impulse equals the change in momentum. On a force vs time graph, the impulse is the area under the curve.

With the frame, the band gives the same force for the same amount of time, so the balloon gets the same impulse and leaves with the same momentum. When people held the band, their arms moved forward and took away some of that push.

Equations

p = mv

J = Favg Δt = Δp

Δp = mv0 − 0  starts at rest

v0 = J / m

All four units together

Hand-held vs. frame

This lab puts everything together. Each shot gets a pullback, an angle, and a height. Energy sets the launch speed, and kinematics gives the landing spot.

For the hand-held launcher, the pullback, angle, and height change a little each shot, like in our trials (heights from 115 to 135 cm). For the frame, they barely change. A shot counts as a hit if it lands within 1 m of the target.

What changes each shot

Hand-held: x1 ± 8%, θ0 ± 6°, h 1.15 to 1.35 m

Frame: x1 ± 1%, θ0 ± 1.5°, h fixed at 1.52 m

These amounts are our estimates for the model. They are not measured data. Our real tests on the frame will replace them.
